The starting position

Most organisations aren't short of AI opinions. They're short of decisions.

The technology has moved faster than the guidance around it. That leaves leadership teams carrying a question they were never given time to answer properly: what should we actually do about this?

01

Expectation has arrived ahead of readiness

Boards, clients and tender documents now ask where you stand on AI. Very few organisations have a position they could defend in detail, and fewer still have one written down.

02

Your people are already using it

Staff are pasting work into AI tools every day, usually without a policy, a standard, or anyone to ask. That is a capability gap and an information risk sitting in the same place.

03

Trials that never become the way work gets done

There is no shortage of experimentation. What tends to be missing is the step where something is chosen, embedded into a real process, and handed to a team who understand it.

04

Advice that never touches your operations

Generic AI thinking is everywhere. Judgement about your workflows, your data, your sector rules and your appetite for risk is considerably harder to come by.

AI has stopped being a technology to keep trialling. It is turning into a capability an organisation either has or doesn't.

The businesses getting real value from it are not the ones with the most tools. They are the ones who decided deliberately where it belonged, built the few things that mattered properly, and made sure their people knew how to use what they'd been given. That is the work we do.

How we work

Understand, plan, implement, educate, improve.

A sequence rather than a package. Some organisations need all five stages. Others already know what they want and need one of them done properly.

  1. 01

    Understand

    We spend time in the detail of how your organisation runs — the processes, the bottlenecks, the systems, the constraints nobody writes down. No AI decision made before this is worth much.

  2. 02

    Plan

    Opportunities are sized, ranked and stress-tested against effort, risk and appetite. You get a short, honest plan — including the ideas we recommend dropping.

  3. 03

    Implement

    We build, configure and integrate — starting with something narrow enough to prove and useful enough to matter. Then we widen it once it holds up in real use.

  4. 04

    Educate

    Your team is brought up to speed on what has changed, how to use it well, and where the boundaries sit. Written standards, practical sessions, plain English.

  5. 05

    Improve

    Adoption is reviewed against what you expected to gain. We adjust, extend where it's working, and retire what isn't. AI capability compounds; one project doesn't.

Common questions

Straight answers before you get in touch.

We don't know where to start. Is that too early to talk?

That's the most common starting point and the one we're built for. The first piece of work is usually understanding your operations well enough to say where AI would and wouldn't help — which is a very different exercise to picking a tool.

Do we need a custom AI system built?

Usually not. For most organisations the gain comes from selecting capable platforms, configuring them properly, redesigning the workflow around them and training people to use them well. We build bespoke when nothing existing fits the requirement, and we'll say so either way.

Is this just automation with an AI label on it?

No. Automation is a specialist capability we bring in when there's genuine repetitive volume to remove. A large share of our work is consulting, solution design and building internal capability — often with no automation involved at all.

How do you handle our data and the risk side?

It's part of the design work rather than a separate exercise. We look at what data a tool would see, where it goes, what should never be shared, where a human has to stay in the loop, and what your people need to be told in writing. Responsible use is a practical constraint, not a policy document nobody reads.

Do you train our staff as well?

Yes — building capability is part of how we work rather than a product sold separately. Sessions are practical and tied to your tools and processes, so people leave able to do the work rather than able to describe the technology.

What size of organisation do you work with?

Mostly SMEs and established mid-sized organisations — typically from a handful of people up to a few hundred. What matters more than headcount is whether there's a decision-maker who wants a straight answer and a team willing to change how something is done.

How does an engagement usually start?

A short conversation, no charge, to understand your position and whether we're the right fit. If it makes sense to continue, we'll scope a defined first piece of work with a fixed price agreed before anything begins.
